# DashCast 1.8.44-beta

Supersedes 1.8.43-beta, which was published an hour earlier and withdrawn. Same fixes, plus one
that did not work, and release notes that told you something untrue about your privacy. Both are
corrected below and I say what went wrong at the end.

> This is a tester pre-release from `audit/integr-1828`. It is not merged into `main` and must not be marked as the repository's Latest release — `1.7.0` remains the stable one.

## Read this first if you have ever sent a bug report

**Wi-Fi network names were leaking.** One report carried 39 of them — the tester's own and their
neighbours'. On the scan lines that also contain a hardware address, the address next to each name
was correctly hidden and only the name survived. The rule expected the car to write `SSID:` and this
car writes `SSID` followed by a space. One character, and every test we had was written against the
form the car never produces. Fixed.

**And Wi-Fi names are not the only thing that has left your car.** The 1.8.43 notes said "position,
VIN and addresses were being removed correctly". That was wrong, and it is the single worst sentence
I have written for this project:

- reports sent **before 1.8.31-beta (16 August)** were not filtered at all;
- reports sent **before 1.8.34-beta (22 August)** still carried your position — three of the four
  forms this car logs were passing through in clear, 229 positions out of 245 across the reports we
  measure against;
- **screenshots have never been filtered**, and still are not — see below.

**On Android 13 cars (DL5.1), reports were never deleted after being sent.** Every report ever
written was still on the car, including old ones from before any of the above was fixed. This
release sweeps them. That means installing it **deletes old, less-redacted reports from your car**,
which is the outcome you want, but you should know it happens.

If reports of yours are sitting in the diagnostics group, this release cannot reach them. Only
deleting the message can.

## Screenshots are not filtered, and the report now says so

If you let a report attach screenshots, the images go out exactly as captured: a map with street
names, a destination, a saved Home pin, a dashcam view. That has always been true, and DashCast asks
you on **every** send — the "Without captures" button is right there. What was wrong is that the
report's own privacy line described only its text, so it read as more thorough than it was. A report
carrying images now states how many and that they are not filtered.

Nothing changed about what is attached. Only about what the report admits to.

## Android Auto should give the centre screen back (untested in a car)

**Before.** Project Android Auto onto the cluster, press Stop, and the centre screen became
unusable: the BYD app drawer showed for a third of a second before Android Auto came back in front,
and the same for the home screen. The only way out was to reboot the head unit.

**What we think was happening.** To make Android Auto reachable on the centre screen again we
relaunch it there. We then try to close it, and on Android Auto that has failed every time we have
seen it — it is a persistent process the car will not let us stop. Since 1.8.30 we deliberately
leave its window in place, because destroying it is what made the app permanently unreachable in an
earlier release. That part was right. What was missing is that we left it in *front*, awake, with
your phone still connected. Our best explanation — **not confirmed on a car** — is that the car's
own Android Auto service then reclaimed the foreground we had handed it.

**Now.** We leave its window where it is and bring the home screen up in front of it.

**When it fires, and when it will not.** The rule is "the app's close could not be confirmed and its
window is on the centre screen" — it keys on that, not on which app it is. In practice that means
Android Auto and probably CarPlay. It does **not** fire if your car falls back to the ADB route
instead of the privileged helper, or if you press Stop while the projection service is unbound. If
you see no change at all, that may be why rather than a failed fix.

**What success and failure look like.** Working: Stop leaves you on the car's home screen. Not
working: Android Auto is in front again within a second. There is also a third outcome — you land on
DashCast's own app list, because the app list is drawn right after the home screen is requested and
may win the race. That one is inconclusive; please say which of the three you saw.

## The rest: reports that answer the question

Five smaller fixes, nearly all the same shape — a section that looked complete and answered nothing
— plus a rework that finally lets those sections be tested.

- The HUD event recorder kept the **oldest** thousand events instead of the newest, so it stopped
  listening 36 seconds into a session and never said so. Newest kept now, and it reports what it
  dropped.
- One sensor pushing 25 times a second was taking 903 of those 1000 lines. Sampled once a second
  now, carrying a count, turning a 36-second window into roughly four and a half minutes.
- The cluster watchdog reported only that it had started; everything it did over the next 90 seconds
  went to a log this car does not carry. Those lines now reach the report, so we can at least tell
  whether DashCast itself was moving windows around.
- Two screen-layer sections never reached the cluster: one ran out on a list of connections before
  reaching any layer, the other on the main display's own layers. The new pass finds the mirror the
  leak check depends on; whether it reaches a cluster layer is **still unproven on this ROM**, so do
  not read a clean section as proof that nothing leaked.
- The report header said "unknown application" about sessions where the projected app was obvious.

The redaction line now names **every** rule, including those that found nothing: `=0` means the rule
ran and found nothing, `=ERR` means it failed. That is what makes a dead rule visible — the previous
line named only rules that had matched, so "no Wi-Fi names here" and "the Wi-Fi rule is dead" printed
identically. One caveat: the four Wi-Fi rules report as a single figure, so a broken one could still
hide behind a working sibling.

## Validation

504 unit tests, all green. Lint unchanged: 14 warnings, all pre-existing in files this release does
not touch, none on any file it does.

Most fixes were checked by removing them again and confirming a test goes red — three of five cases
for one, five of six for another. **Three could not be**: the watchdog's new log lines, the sensor
throttle's wiring, and the home-screen restore's wiring have no test that fails if they are deleted.
Those rest on review alone.

The redaction fix was verified end to end by running the real report through the shipped code: 59
occurrences covering all 39 distinct names removed, and no network name left in any form we know of.

An adversarial review of the Android Auto fix found three real defects in the first version,
including one where two parts of the code decided the same thing from two different observations and
could disagree. All three were fixed before this release.

- Version code `634`, version name `1.8.44-beta`.
- APK: `DashCast-v1.8.44-beta-debug.apk`, `16,881,079` bytes.
- APK SHA-256: `d15192453373d0a676ff56d2e318c615436c68cd6654599ca11bf4f25b4504b6`.
- Signing certificate SHA-256 (bydPlatform): `c8a2e9bccf597c2fb6dc66bee293fc13f2fc47ec77bc6b2b0d52c11f51192ab8`.
- `assembleDebug` and `assembleRelease` (R8) both green. Release APK scanned: the only
  credential-shaped match is a library's PEM header literal, with no key material in the file.

**The version number matters this time.** Three of these fixes run inside the privileged helper,
which only replaces itself when the version code changes. A build still numbered 632 would have kept
the old helper and silently dropped them.

**Nothing here has run in a car.**

## Why 1.8.43 was withdrawn

Two mistakes, both of the kind this release is full of fixes for.

The cluster-layer pattern in 1.8.43 matched nothing. SurfaceFlinger pads that field on this ROM and
the pattern did not allow for it — and I had "verified by simulation" against a sample dump I wrote
myself, unpadded. The check confirmed my own assumption instead of the car.

And the privacy sentence above went out because I published before an adversarial check of the notes
had finished. It came back afterwards and caught it, along with several smaller overstatements now
corrected here. The check was the right idea; publishing without waiting for it was not.

## If something goes wrong

Send a bug report and say what the car was doing. Reinstalling 1.8.42-beta is safe.
