# DashCast 1.9.19-beta — Experimental external app video

Receive video from an app running on the external Android box through
**[DashSat 0.7.0-dev](https://github.com/Kiroha/dashcast-satellite/releases/tag/v0.7.0-dev)**.
The app and screen capture run on the box. DashCast receives, decodes and displays a single
WebRTC video track through its existing main-display preview or ready cluster projection.

**Vehicle APK: versionName `1.9.19-beta`, versionCode `658`.** Install over the existing
DashCast application without uninstalling. The package, platform signing identity, pairing,
protocol v1 and already working Android 10 TLS identity remain compatible. This pre-release
continues from `feat/hud-2.0`; **1.9.0 remains the stable release**.

## Try the external video path while parked

1. Start the DashSat connection on the Tbox.
2. Open **Preview received map** in DashCast, or **Show on cluster** after the existing cluster
   projection is ready. Video preview does not activate a second OEM projection sequence.
3. In DashSat, open **Video projection · experimental**, choose an app, and tap **Start video sharing**.
4. Grant fresh Android screen-sharing consent. On Android 14+, select the same app in Android's
   own sharing chooser. Earlier devices share the entire display. The app opens on the box.
5. Check the image, then use **Stop video sharing** or its notification Stop action when finished.

Video remains separate from guidance. The user has confirmed synchronized official Google Maps
guidance on Tbox Ultra 1 / Android 15 with DashCast on DiLink 3 / Android 10. That feedback does
not validate vehicle video, recovery scenarios or measured latency.

## Receiver behavior

- Keep one asynchronous stats request in flight and independently clear a frozen image after
  six seconds without decoder/presented-frame progress. Incoming packets and control pings
  never establish fresh video. Old stats/SDP callbacks cannot affect a replacement session.
- Keep the cluster viewer tied to its original resolved display and the existing active
  ClusterService projection. Projection/display loss closes its decoder; main-display preview
  remains independent. Daemon ownership, OEM restoration and existing restrictions are preserved.
- Optionally long-press the main-display preview to enable a small performance overlay, then
  open the cluster viewer. It shows available codec, resolution, FPS, bitrate, dropped frames
  and average decode time. Power-efficient decoder status is shown only when WebView supplies
  it; it does not certify hardware decoding or measure GPU workload.

The vehicle continues using its installed WebView for WebRTC. No native codec AAR, screen capture,
microphone, remote input, new vehicle transport or driver restriction bypass is added to DashCast.

## CPU/GPU goal and validation limits

The goal is lower vehicle CPU/GPU workload than running the navigation app in the car. Video
must still be decoded and composed in the vehicle; it is not a zero-cost compressed-video relay.
DashSat starts at up to 960×540, 15 fps and 1.5 Mbps with hardware encoding on the box, H.264
preferred. Hardware codec support in other car apps does not prove WebView's decoder selection.

Compare identical DashCast idle, local Maps and external-video scenarios with Overdrive recording,
first for ten minutes, then thirty sustained minutes. Record screen-to-cluster latency, codec/decoder
evidence, CPU/GPU, heat and frame loss. Actual vehicle video and CPU/GPU savings remain pending;
JVM, JavaScript and emulator checks cannot establish those results.
See [receiver acceptance](../satellite/EXTERNAL_VIDEO_VALIDATION.md) and the companion's
[setup/performance checklist](https://github.com/Kiroha/dashcast-satellite/blob/main/docs/EXTERNAL_VIDEO.md).
