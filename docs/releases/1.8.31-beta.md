# DashCast 1.8.31-beta

The app now asks before any diagnostic leaves your car, filters what does leave, and no longer
carries a credential anyone could pull out of the APK.

> This is a tester pre-release from `switch-kotlin`. It is not merged into `main` and must not be marked as the repository's Latest release — `1.7.0` remains the stable one.

## Read this first: 1.8.29 and 1.8.30 can no longer send anything

The Telegram token that older versions used has been revoked. It had to be: every published APK
since versionCode 441 carried it inside the binary, extractable by anyone who downloaded a release.

The consequence is immediate. If you are on 1.8.29 or 1.8.30, your bug reports and HUD reports no
longer reach anyone — they fall back to a local save and the share sheet. Nothing is lost, but
nothing arrives either. **This version is the fix, and there is no way back to the old channel.**

## What you will see first: a question

At your first attempt to send a report, DashCast asks once whether diagnostics may leave the car,
and shows what a report contains. In your language — all thirteen the app ships.

It is asked then, and not when you install, because that is the only moment the question means
anything: you have just described a problem and you know why you are being asked.

**Either answer lets you finish.** If you refuse, the report is still captured and offered to the
share sheet, so you keep it and decide yourself what happens to it — and the app now tells you
that, with the file name, instead of opening a share sheet over a screen that still says "Sending".

You can re-read the notice and change your mind at any time: **Settings → Bug report delivery**.

## What a report actually contains

The notice lists it, and the list was built by reading the collection code and then measuring it
against 160 real reports, not from memory. Three things came out of that measurement:

- **75% of real reports carried at least one identifier** nobody meant to collect. The vehicle
  serial number was in more than half of them, put there by the car's own telemetry where our log
  capture picks it up.
- **Screenshots are on by default**, and an earlier version of this notice implied they were not.
  They are full pictures of the centre screen and the cluster — they can show a map with your
  destination. A picture cannot be filtered the way text can, which is why you are still asked
  separately at every send.
- Some reports carried **the names and hardware addresses of phones connected to the car's
  hotspot** — passengers, who never saw any notice.

Before a report is sent, DashCast now removes the vehicle serial number, Wi-Fi network names,
hardware addresses, positions, e-mail addresses and account names. This applies to bug reports, HUD
reports and diagnostic bundles alike.

What remains is copied as it is: the DashCast log and the Android system log, which hold whatever
every other app on the car wrote. We do not filter that, and we cannot know in advance what is in
it. The notice says so.

## Why the filtering is narrow on purpose

The obvious rules are the dangerous ones. A pattern matching any seventeen-character vehicle number
fires 1370 times on those 160 reports and **never once on a vehicle number** — it eats display
identifiers, the field every cluster diagnosis in this project is made with. A pattern matching
decimal coordinates is 92% window-manager scaling values.

So every rule is anchored on the key that names the value, and every one was checked both ways:
what it removes, and what it must not touch. Display identifiers, window scales, broadcast
addresses, install paths and the loopback line that diagnoses a dead daemon all come through
untouched, and there are tests that go red if that stops being true.

## The token is out of the binary

Uploads now go through a relay that holds the credential server-side. A decompiled APK yields an
HTTPS address and nothing else: it can be used to send something to the relay and for nothing else
— no reading of anyone's report, no acting as the bot.

The practical gain is not secrecy, it is that the credential can now be changed in seconds without
reaching a single car. Which is what made revoking the old one possible at all.

## Also in this release

- The user manual claimed, in thirteen languages, that reports contain no position and no app
  content. That was not true and has been corrected.
- Sharing the raw log from the Log screen sends it **unfiltered** — the redaction runs on reports,
  not there. The manual now says so.
- The extraction tool's privacy page said it collects no personal data. It writes an inventory of
  every application installed on the car. The page now says that too.
- The opt-in for raw navigation text did not actually gate it: one log line printed the whole
  notification — street, destination, ETA — and only when parsing failed, which is exactly when a
  report gets sent.
- A long description in the report form could silently kill the upload. Fixed.
- Sending could freeze the wizard for over four minutes with every control disabled. Now at most
  about a minute, and it tells you what happened.

## What is not fixed

Screenshots are still full unblurred frames; there is no image redaction and this release does not
pretend otherwise. The report still carries a list of the applications installed on your car. And
two of the four diagnostic screens still require a trip to Settings to answer the consent question
the first time — they tell you so, but they cannot ask it themselves yet.

## Verification

368 unit tests. `assembleDebug`, `assembleRelease` with R8, and lint all green with zero errors and
zero missing translations. The release APK was scanned: **no credential pattern in any dex or
resource**, which is the claim this whole release rests on.

Not validated on a car. The reporting path changed substantially; if a report does not arrive,
please say so through any channel — including the share sheet, which now always works.
